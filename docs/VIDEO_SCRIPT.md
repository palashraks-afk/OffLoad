# Video script for the Congressional App Challenge (about 2 minutes 40)

The rules ask for 1 to 3 minutes, public on YouTube or Vimeo, and the video must say your name, the app's name, what the app is for in ONE clear sentence, who it is for, the tools and coding languages, and show the app working. This script has all of that. You do not have to show your face. A screen recording with your voice is fine.

## Where and how to record
1. **At home, in a quiet room.** Close the door and turn off fans, TV and notifications. If you have earbuds with a microphone, wear them. They sound better than a laptop mic.
2. **On your computer, in Chrome.** Open https://palashraks-afk.github.io/ShiftReady/ . Press F12, click the little phone icon at the top left of the panel, and pick "iPhone 12 Pro" or similar so the app looks like a phone. Close the panel (F12 again) once the phone view is set. Browser zoom 100%.
3. **Record screen and voice.** On Windows press Windows + Alt + R to start and stop (Xbox Game Bar). Click the Chrome window first. The file saves in Videos\Captures. If that does not work, press Windows + G, then click the Record button. Do a 10 second test and play it back to check you can hear yourself.
4. **Read the script out loud once without recording**, then record. Two or three takes are normal. Slow down; a calm voice sounds better than a fast one. Pause for a second after each tap so people can see what changed.
5. **Upload to YouTube.** Choose Visibility: Public (not Unlisted, not Private). Copy the link. Open it in a private window to check it plays with sound. Paste it into the form.
6. **Check the length.** Under 3:00. If it runs long, cut the Employers part first.

## The script (say it in your own words; keep every fact)
**0:00  Start on the first screen (the dark banner with 0%)**
> Hi, I'm Palash Rakshit, a student at Redlands High School in California's 23rd district, and this is Shift Ready.

**0:10  Still on the first screen**
> Shift Ready is a free web app that checks whether a worker can get to a shift and back home by bus or train, and helps workers, managers and cities ask for better service when they can't.

**0:25  Point at the 0% and the 11 PM tick**
> I used to ride home from soccer practice and pass bus stops with workers waiting at them. Later I passed a memorial near my house for a person who died in a hit-and-run at night. I started wondering how people get home once the buses stop running. In the San Bernardino County part of our district, about one in five people live within a five minute walk of a bus at noon. At 10 PM it's under two percent, and by 11 PM no neighborhood has a bus within that walk.

**0:55  Tap the example called Night shift (Yucaipa to Loma Linda University Health)**
> Here's a night shift. I live in Yucaipa, I start at Loma Linda University Health at 10 PM, and I finish at 6:30 in the morning. The app checks the real timetables and says Partly: there's a bus, but the walk is long. This is the weather where I'd be waiting. This map shows every leg of the trip. This is the ride home after my shift, and this is the next bus if I miss one.

**1:20  Tap Message my boss, then Ask for better service**
> One tap writes a short note to my boss with the bus I can actually catch. Another writes a request to the transit agency, using these numbers.

**1:30  Go back and tap Early hospital shift, then switch the language to Spanish**
> Now an early hospital shift in Redlands. This one says Yes, you can. And the whole app works in Spanish.

**1:42  Open the Map tab and drag the hour slider to 11 PM**
> The Map tab shows hour by hour where service disappears. At 11 PM the stops go quiet.

**1:52  Open the Employers tab**
> The Employers tab is for managers. It asks which start times your workers can reach, then gives actions and a one page brief for the transit agency.

**2:03  Change the region to Denver or Seattle**
> It isn't only my district. The same method works in twelve places, from Sacramento to Seattle, and the data is free to download.

**2:12  Back to the first screen**
> It's built in JavaScript and Python, with public timetables from the transit agencies, Census data, federal crash data and OpenStreetMap. It's for shift workers who don't drive or don't want to drive tired, the managers who schedule them, and the city and transit staff who plan service. I used an AI tool, Claude Code, and it wrote most of the current code. The idea, the plan, the data and the decisions about what the app says are mine, and every session is logged publicly.

**2:40  Last screen**
> Next I want to add live delays and test it with real workers. I didn't find another free tool that starts from the end of a shift and checks the ride home. Thank you for watching.

## Facts you are saying (check them on the live app the day you record)
- About one in five people near a bus at noon, under 2% at 10 PM, none within a five minute walk at 11 PM (San Bernardino County part of CA-23, 729,052 people).
- Twelve places work in the app.
- The Night shift example says Partly; the Early hospital shift example says Yes.

## Do not say
- "Saves lives," "prevents crashes," "the first," "nobody has done this."
- Any number that is not on the screen or in this script.
- That DC is in the app (it is not).

## Before you record: three people test (makes the video and your answers stronger)
Ask 3 people (a family member who takes the bus, a friend with a part-time job, a manager if you know one). For each: 5 minutes, you only watch.
1. Give them the link and say: "Pretend you start work at Loma Linda University Health at 10 PM. See if you can get there and home." Do not help.
2. Write down where they hesitated, what they said, and whether they could read the answer.
3. Ask: "Would you use this? What is missing?"
Write the results in docs/SHIFTREADY_TESTING.md with first names and the date. Use only what really happened. If it happened, you can add one true line to the video, like "Three people tried it, and two understood the answer without help."
