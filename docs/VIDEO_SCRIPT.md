# Video plan for the Congressional App Challenge (about 2 minutes 40)

Rules: 1 to 3 minutes, public link starting with https (YouTube is easiest), show the app working, say your name, the app's name and what it is for. Only say things that are true for you. Numbers below are from the live app on 2026-10-25; if the app changes, read the numbers off the screen on the day you record.

## How to record (easiest way)
1. Open https://palashraks-afk.github.io/OffLoad/ in Chrome. Press F12, click the phone icon (device toolbar) and pick a phone size, so the app looks like it does on a phone. Or just use the app on your actual phone.
2. Screen record with your voice:
   - Windows: press Windows + Alt + R (Xbox Game Bar), or Windows + G and click Record.
   - iPhone: Control Center, Screen Recording (turn the microphone on). Android: Quick Settings, Screen record.
3. Do two practice takes before the real one. Speak slowly. Cut mistakes by recording again; a short, clear video beats a perfect one.
4. Upload to YouTube, set Visibility to **Public** (not "Unlisted" and not "Private"), copy the link. Open the link in a private window to check it plays and has sound.
5. Paste the link into the application form.

## Script (read it in your own words; the [brackets] are yours)
**0:00 Who and what (10 sec).** "Hi, I'm Palash Rakshit from Redlands High School. This is Shift Ready, my app for California's 23rd district."

**0:10 Why (15 sec).** "I used to ride home from soccer practice and pass bus stops with workers waiting at them. [Add one true detail.] I started wondering how they got home once the buses stopped running."

**0:25 The problem in one sentence (15 sec).** "In the San Bernardino County part of our district, about one in five people live within a five-minute walk of a bus at noon, but none do at 11 PM. A worker whose shift ends at night can't get home, and nothing tells them, or their boss, before they take the job."
(Show the first screen with the big "0%".)

**0:40 Demo (50 sec).** Tap the "Night shift, no car" example (Yucaipa to Loma Linda University Health).
- "It checks the real timetable. The answer is Partly: there is a bus, but the walk is long."
- Point at the weather row: "It shows the weather and air quality where I'd be waiting."
- Point at the trip line, then the ride home: "It also checks the ride home after the shift ends."
- Point at "If you miss it": "and the next bus, if the first one doesn't come."
- Tap "Message my boss": "One tap writes a short, factual note for my manager."
- Switch to Spanish: "It also works in Spanish."
Then tap "Early hospital shift" to show a Yes result in a few seconds.

**1:30 Map (15 sec).** Open the Map tab, drag the hour slider to 11 PM: "Watch the stops go quiet as the night goes on."

**1:45 For employers (15 sec).** Open the Employers tab: "Employers and cities can see which shift start times their workers can actually reach."

**2:00 Works elsewhere (10 sec).** Change the region to Denver or Atlanta: "The same method works in nine places, from San Bernardino to Seattle and Boston, using each agency's public timetable."

**2:10 How it was built, and AI (25 sec).** "It's a web app in JavaScript and Python, with public data from the transit agencies, the Census, the traffic-safety administration and OpenStreetMap. I used an AI assistant, Claude Code, and it wrote most of the code. [Say what you did yourself: for example, I chose the problem and the district, decided what the app should and should not claim, and tested it. Only say what is true.] Everything the AI did is logged in the project."

**2:35 What's next (15 sec).** "Next: live delays, testing it with real workers, and sending the numbers to Omnitrans. Thank you."

## Mistakes to avoid
- Don't say it "saves lives," "prevents crashes" or "is the first." Say "we didn't find another tool that does all of this."
- Don't read a number that is not on the screen.
- Don't skip the AI sentence; the rules require it, and it must match what you put in the form.
- Keep it under 3:00.

## Three people test (do this before you record; it makes the video and your answers much stronger)
Ask 3 people (a family member who takes the bus, a friend with a part-time job, a manager if you know one). For each: 5 minutes, you only watch.
1. Give them the link and say: "Pretend you start work at [place] at 10 PM. See if you can get there." Don't help.
2. Write down where they hesitated, what they said, whether they could read the answer.
3. Ask: "Would you use this? What's missing?"
Write the results in docs/SHIFTREADY_TESTING.md with first names and the date. Use only what really happened. One true sentence like "3 people tried it, and 2 understood the answer without help" is worth more than any statistic.
