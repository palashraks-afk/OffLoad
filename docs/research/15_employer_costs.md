# 15. What lateness and turnover cost employers (read 2026-10-25)

**1. Cost of replacing an employee (Center for American Progress, 2012).** Read: https://www.americanprogress.org/article/there-are-significant-business-costs-to-replacing-employees/ (Boushey and Glynn, 16 November 2012).
- Median cost of turnover: 21% of annual salary for all jobs except executives and physicians (27 case studies); 20% for jobs paying under $50,000 (22 case studies); 16% under $30,000.
- Range across studies: 5.8% to 213%. Eleven research papers; the page is inconsistent about the count of case studies (30 or 31) and the years covered, and notes a correction without saying what changed. Only 2 of 11 papers included indirect costs.
- Limits: not about transportation specifically; studies are from 1992-2007 or later; costs vary by industry and region.

**2. Unscheduled absence "costs $3,600 per hourly employee per year."** I could not trace this to an original study. The earliest appearance I found (via search summary) is a trade newsletter from 1 November 2005 about Kronos software, with no cited study; later sites attribute it to Circadian Technologies without a report I could open. **Discarded; the app and videos should not quote it.**

**3. Transportation as a stated barrier to work.** In a 2022 South Carolina survey, almost 20 percent of people who were able to work but not working cited transportation as a barrier (Richmond Fed, 2023, read earlier in this project). One state; not about shifts.

**4. Worker surveys on lateness (via search summary only; not opened).**
- MassBio 2019: among transit riders in that survey, 79% had been late for work in the past month because of delays; 60% of respondents would change jobs for a better commute. Industry-specific (biotech) and self-selected.
- A 2008 survey (BusinessWeek/TransitCenter-era): 24% arrived at least 15 minutes late 3+ days a month because of the commute.
Do not quote these in the app without opening the originals.

**5. Employer evidence the app would like to have and does not.** I found no study that measures how often missed shifts at warehouses or hospitals are caused by bus schedules. A Lorain County, Ohio transit survey of employers launched in December 2025 (via search summary) has no published results.

## What the app can honestly say
- Replacing a worker typically costs a fifth of the salary (CAP median), so a schedule that loses workers is not free.
- It cannot give a dollar figure for missed shifts caused by buses.
