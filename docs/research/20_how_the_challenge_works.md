# How the Congressional App Challenge works (read 2026-10-25)

Sources read in this session: the official 2026 rules PDF (https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-CAC-Rules.pdf, sections 7 to 9), the official FAQ for Members' offices (https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/congress/frequently-asked-questions/), the 2025 recap (https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/record-breaking-year-for-congressional-innovation-2025-challenge-recap/), the #HouseOfCode page (https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/students/houseofcode/), the 2023 CA-23 winner page (https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/23-ca23/) and Rep. Obernolte's office page (https://obernolte.house.gov/services/congressional-app-challenge).

## There are no national rounds. The decision is made in your district.
- Each Member of Congress hosts their own district competition. The national team (the Internet Education Foundation, for the House) runs the platform and recruits backup judges; it does not rank apps nationally.
- **Who judges (rules 7.1):** "Demonstration videos and submission answers will be reviewed and evaluated by the Member of Congress and their office. Members may opt to use a panel of judges."
- **CA-23 specifically:** Rep. Obernolte's office page says a panel of local judges who work in software and entrepreneurial fields judges the district's submissions; in May 2025 he described the winners as "selected by our expert panel of judges." The judges are not named on the page.
- The FAQ says panels should have an odd number of judges (for ties), be free of conflicts of interest, and have basic programming familiarity (examples: a university computer science professor or student, a tech entrepreneur, a tech company employee). Judges receive the demonstration video links and a grading rubric the office chooses; they do not get the student information sheet.

## What they judge (rules 7.2)
a. Quality of the idea (including creativity and originality).
b. Implementation of the idea (including user experience and design).
c. Demonstrated excellence of coding and programming skills.
Judges can request the app and source code to verify it works as stated; refusing means disqualification (7.3). The Member's office may change the judging process or criteria at any time; judging is "fair and impartial but left entirely to the discretion of the Member" (7.4). Odds depend on the number and quality of submissions (7.5). The administrators' decisions are binding and final (rules section 4).
What this means: judges see first your **video and your written answers**, not the live app. The video's job is to make the idea clear in under three minutes and show it working; the answers' job is to show effort and understanding. Be ready for a request to explain or run the code.

## Timeline
- Submissions close Monday 2026-10-26 at 12:00 pm Eastern (9:00 am Pacific).
- District winners are chosen by the Member's office; in 2025 the national recap said Members select winners between November 15 and December 31. Winners are announced by the Member; the national team may not reveal results before the embargo on **2027-01-15** (rules 8).
- #HouseOfCode, the demo celebration at the U.S. Capitol, is held each spring (the 2026 one drew over 1,000 attendees; next is Spring 2027).

## Prizes (rules 9, FAQ, CA-23 page)
- The winning app in each district is featured on House.gov (the FAQ says about 10 million visitors a year) and on CongressionalAppChallenge.us, and is **eligible to be displayed in the U.S. Capitol** (the application form says for a year).
- Winners are **invited to #HouseOfCode** to demo for their Representatives. The FAQ says the students pay their own travel; Member offices cannot use their allowance for student travel.
- A **copyright registration fee waiver** through the ARTS Act.
- District offices may give gifts or awards of nominal value (certificates, folders, frames). The administrator may distribute additional prizes equally to all districts. **No cash prize is stated in the rules.**
- Hack Club's Congressional Certification (the opt-in on the form) is separate: free recognition for open-source apps and a hackathon invitation.

## Odds and context
- 2025 national totals: 13,830 students, 4,650 apps, 394 districts (all records). That is about 12 apps per district on average; 16 Members received more than 50 submissions each. CA-23's own count is not published.
- 56% of 2025 teams used or integrated AI tools; the application form says nearly 70% of last year's winners used AI. Using AI is allowed if fully disclosed and it does not make up the entirety of the technical work (rules, "AI USAGE").
- 70% of 2025 apps focused on civic engagement or social good; 33% of participants were beginners.
- CA-23's 2023 winner was *The Newcomer*, a game by Darren Xu, a 12th grader at Redlands High School (the student's own school). The 2025 CA-23 winners were announced by Rep. Obernolte on X; I did not find their names.

## What this changes in the plan
1. Spend the most effort on the **video** (the idea in one sentence, working demo, honest AI line) and the **seven written answers**; those are what judges read.
2. The coding criterion is the weakest part of an AI-assisted project, so be ready to explain how the planner works in your own words (see docs/CODE_WALKTHROUGH.md if it exists) and to show the source and the AI log. Do not claim code you cannot show.
3. A local panel of software and entrepreneurial people will respond to a clear real-world problem, a working product, and honesty. Say what it does not do.
