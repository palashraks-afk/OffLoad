# 14. Walking to and waiting at stops: safety (read 2026-10-25)

**1. Midblock bus stops and fatal pedestrian crashes (Rewalt, Brakewood, Cherry, 2025).** Read the journal listing: https://www.worldtransitresearch.info/research/11390 - "An analysis of pedestrian safety at bus stops using FARS data," *Journal of Safety Research* 95, 147-159 (2025).
- Compares fatal pedestrian crashes related to transit bus stops with other fatal pedestrian crashes within a quarter mile and half mile.
- Result (abstract): "midblock crashes had 4.7 to 5.2 times the odds of being transit bus stop-related."
- **Correct reading:** this is an odds ratio for a bus-stop-related crash being at a midblock location compared with the nearby comparison crashes. It is not "midblock stops are 4.7 times as deadly" and not a death rate per stop. The page did not give data years or crash counts. The earlier, looser phrasing ("deaths up to 5 times higher") should not be used.
- Also from the abstract (via the listing): most fatal crashes in both groups occurred midblock, on higher-speed roads, on arterials.

**2. Speed and the chance a pedestrian dies (AAA Foundation, Tefft, 2011).** Read: https://aaafoundation.org/impact-speed-pedestrians-risk-severe-injury-death/
- Average risk of death for a pedestrian struck by a car or light truck: 10% at 23 mph, 25% at 32 mph, 50% at 42 mph, 75% at 50 mph, 90% at 58 mph.
- Data: US crashes 1994-1998 (weighted for over-sampling of severe cases), standardized to the average pedestrian in 2007-2009. The page gives no sample size (a search summary says 422 pedestrians; not verified).
- Limits: old crash data, vehicles and medical care have changed; European studies differ.

**3. When pedestrians die (IIHS, 2024 data).** Read: https://www.iihs.org/topics/fatality-statistics/detail/pedestrians
- 7,080 pedestrian deaths in 2024. About 73% occurred between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m.; about 49% between 9 p.m. and 6 a.m. By road type: other major roads 62%, interstates and freeways 16%, minor roads 20%.
- Limits: the page reports time of day, not "in the dark" directly. IIHS has said earlier that roughly 75% of 2022 deaths were in the dark (via search summary; not read).

**4. Lighting.** Searches turned up a Delhi case-control study (well-lit bus stops protective at night; via search summary only) and no US evaluation of stop lighting that I read. **Not established for this region.** Our own OpenStreetMap check found only 0.4% of road segments in central San Bernardino carry a "lit" tag, which says the tag is rarely filled in, not that streets are dark.

**5. Local count.** The app counts pedestrian and cyclist deaths from NHTSA FARS 2019-2023 inside each district (201 inside CA-23).

## What the app can honestly say
- Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark and on major roads (IIHS 2024); the chance of dying rises steeply with speed (AAA); bus-stop-related fatal crashes are more likely to be midblock (2025 study, odds language).
- It cannot say that any particular stop is unsafe, or that using a lit stop would prevent harm.
