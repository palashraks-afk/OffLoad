# 16. The Inland Empire and San Bernardino County (read 2026-10-25)

**1. Omnitrans service plan FY2026 (7 May 2025).** Read: https://omnitrans.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Service-Plan-FY-2026.pdf (text extracted from the PDF; its internal title metadata says "Fiscal year 2011 Service Element", a template leftover).
- Service restoration: service levels grew from 83% (May 2024) to 86% (August 2024), 96% (January 2025), and 100% of the planned "ConnectForward" fixed-route level at the May 2025 service change.
- Ridership: about 8.0 million boardings in FY2025 (up 14.7% on FY2024); about 23,000 average weekday fixed-route boardings (22,000 in FY2024); six "core" routes carry 58% of ridership at 16.2 passengers per revenue hour; projected 8.65 million in FY2026.
- Resources: revenue hours 661,000 (FY2025) rising to 718,000 (FY2026); operating costs $99.9 million (FY2025) and $112.3 million (FY2026). Dividing gives roughly $151 per revenue hour for FY2025 (my arithmetic, all services including demand response) versus the $141 per revenue hour (2022, National Transit Database) the app uses; the app's figure is older.
- Route 300 (a first/last-mile shuttle between the San Bernardino Transit Center and downtown employment) was proposed for elimination: it carried fewer than three passengers per weekday across 25 trips despite employer-targeted marketing. A relevant caution about shuttles that serve one stretch.
- The plan describes full service as the 2023-2030 plan level; it contains no late-night "owl" service. (Consistent with our timetable analysis.)
- Free Fares for School (K-12) transactions rose by a monthly average of 27% compared with the prior year.

**2. Commute figures (San Bernardino County Community Indicators, ACS 2024).** Read: https://indicators.sbcounty.gov/transportation/mobility/ - average commute 32.8 minutes (California 29.7, US 27.2); 74.5% drove alone (down from 80.2% ten years earlier); working from home rose from 4.3% in 2015.

**4. Transit-job access in the metro.** Brookings (2011): see doc 12. The New Geography summary puts the Riverside-San Bernardino metro lowest of 29 large metros on 45-minute job access (1.3%); verify on the Brookings tables before use.

**6. Vanpool subsidy (SBCTA SB Loop).** Read: https://www.gosbcta.com/vanpool-subsidies-increase-in-the-inland-empire/ (7 August 2023): the subsidy for regular vanpools into Riverside or San Bernardino counties "is now $600 (up from $400)"; zero-emission vans may qualify for up to $700 (previously $500). ridetheloop.com (read) advertises "up to a $600 subsidy." Neither page I read stated seat counts, mileage, or occupancy rules, and the phone number the app previously showed, (833) 743-3742, is not on either page (the site lists 909-386-2849 and 833-743-3843 with a temporary connectivity notice). **The app text was corrected to cite only the $600 figure and point to ridetheloop.com.** An earlier research note (docs/ANGLES_TO_STRENGTHEN.md) carries the older rule details and phone number; treat them as unverified.

## What the app can honestly say
- Omnitrans reached 100% of its planned service in May 2025 and ridership is growing; the planned service has no overnight network, so night gaps are structural, not a post-pandemic leftover.
- County commutes are longer than the state and national averages.
