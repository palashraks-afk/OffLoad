# 2026 Congressional App Challenge: rules that matter (read the official PDF yourself)

Source: https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-CAC-Rules.pdf (read 2026-10-12)

## The two clauses that change how this project must be built
- **ORIGINALITY**: the app "must be original and solely created by the contestant. All coding and technical development must be done by the student or student team." Open-source libraries are allowed if documented, and the project must show significant personal effort and technical understanding.
- **AI USAGE**: AI tools are permitted if **all AI usage is fully disclosed** in the submission, but AI "may only be used to support specific aspects of the project and must not constitute the entirety of the technical development." Participants must demonstrate significant individual contributions and technical understanding.
- Judges can ask for access to the app and source code to check it works as described; refusing means disqualification.

**Consequence for this repo:** the OffloadWatch code in this repository was written almost entirely by an AI assistant (Claude Code). It must NOT be submitted as the student's own work. Treat it as a reference/learning artifact. Whatever is submitted must be coded by the student, with any AI help limited to specific supporting tasks and disclosed.

## Other rules
- Deadline: **Monday 2026-10-26, 12:00 pm Eastern** (some House pages list other times; confirm with the district office).
- Student enrolled in middle or high school on 2026-10-26; U.S. resident; enter in home district OR school district (not both). Individual or team up to 4.
- Apps created after **2025-10-30**. A "2.0" of an earlier app may only highlight the new coding.
- One app per person per year.
- Demonstration video **1-3 minutes**, public on YouTube or Vimeo. It must state: participant names, app name, the purpose in ONE clear sentence, target audience, tools and languages used, and show the app working. Over-length videos may be penalized.
- Judging (per the rulebook): quality of the idea (creativity, originality); implementation (user experience, design); demonstrated excellence of coding. The Member of Congress may modify the process at any time. Odds depend on the number of eligible submissions.
- Winners announced by the Member; embargo until 2027-01-15.

## Disclosure checklist for the submission
- List every library, dataset and service used.
- State exactly where AI tools were used (for example: "explained a concept", "reviewed a function", "suggested a test") and what the student wrote.
- Be ready to explain every line of the submitted code on request.
